## Core Idea
Vascular Plant Structure, Growth, and Development establishes that biological function emerges from regulated interactions across scales: plants have a hierarchical organization. Structure-function correlations and evolutionary adaptations dictate organismal survival and thermodynamic stability.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Transpiration-Cohesion-Tension Xylem Transport**
  - **When to use**: Explaining long-distance negative-pressure water ascent in vascular plants
  - **How**: Evaporative water loss at stomata creates negative hydrostatic pressure (tension) in leaf mesophyll; hydrogen bonding cohesion transmits this pull downward through continuous xylem vessels.
- **Pressure-Flow Translocation in Phloem**
  - **When to use**: Explaining positive-pressure bulk flow of photoassimilates from source to sink
  - **How**: Active loading of sucrose at source tissues lowers water potential, drawing water into sieve tubes; high turgor pressure pushes sap toward sinks where sucrose is unloaded.

## Key Concepts
### Concept 35.1: Plants have a hierarchical organization
- Va s c u l a r p l a n t s h ave s h o o t s c o n s i st i n g o f stems, leaves, and, in angiosperms, flowers. Roots anchor the plant, absorb and conduct water and minerals, and store food. Leaves are attached to stem nodes and are the main organs of photosynthesis. The axillary buds, in axils of leaves and stems, give rise to branches. Plant organs may be adapted for specialized functions.
- V ascular plants have three tissue systems—dermal, vascular, and ground—which are continuous throughout the plant. The dermal tissue is a continuous layer of cells that covers the plant exterior. V ascular tissues (xylem and phloem) facilitate the long-distance transport of substances. Ground tissues function in storage, metabolism, and regeneration.
- Parenchyma cells are relatively undifferentiated and thin-walled cells that retain the ability to divide; they perform most of the metabolic functions of synthesis and storage. Collenchyma cells have unevenly thickened walls; they support young, growing parts of the plant. Sclerenchyma cells—sclereids and fibers—have thick, lignified walls that help support mature, nongrowing parts of the plant. Tracheid s and vessel elements, the water- conducting cells of xylem, have thick walls and are dead at functional maturity. Sieve-tube elements are living but highly modified cells that are largely devoid of internal organelles; they function in the transport of sugars through the phloem of angiosperms.

### Concept 35.5: Growth, morphogenesis, and cell
- Cell division and cell expansion are the primary determinants of growth.
- Morphogenesis, the development of body shape and organization, depends on cells responding to positional information from their neighbors.
- Cell differentiation, arising from differential gene activation, enables cells within the plant to assume different functions despite having identical genomes. The way in which a plant cell differentiates is determined largely by the cell’s position in the developing plant.
- Internal or environmental cues may cause a plant to switch from one developmental stage to another—for example, from developing juvenile leaves to developing mature leaves. Such changes in shape are called phase changes.
- Studies of floral development have provided a model system for studying pattern formation. The ABC hypothesis identifies how three classes of genes control formation of sepals, petals, stamens, and carpels.

## Mental Models
- Think of water potential $\Psi = \Psi_s + \Psi_p$ as a thermodynamic potential energy gradient; water ALWAYS flows spontaneously from higher $\Psi$ to lower $\Psi$.
- Use the primary meristem vs secondary meristem model: apical meristems lengthen shoots and roots; lateral meristems (vascular and cork cambium) thicken woody axes.
- Think of plant defense as a two-tier immune system: PAMP-triggered immunity (surface recognition) and Effector-triggered immunity (intracellular hypersensitive response & systemic acquired resistance).

## Anti-patterns
- **Believing Plants Push Water from Roots (Capillarity Fallacy)**: Root pressure rarely exceeds 0.2 MPa and cannot push water to treetop heights; transpiration pull generates negative pressures up to -2.0 MPa to lift water 100 meters.
- **Confusing Photoperiod Day Length with Night Length**: Flowering in short-day and long-day plants is strictly controlled by the duration of the UNINTERRUPTED dark period (critical night length).
- **Assuming All Plant Cells Have Chloroplasts**: Root cells, internal stem parenchyma, and floral petals lack functional chloroplasts and rely entirely on sucrose translocated from photosynthetic leaves.
