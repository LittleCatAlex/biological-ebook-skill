## Core Idea
Phylogeny and the Tree of Life establishes that biological function emerges from regulated interactions across scales: phylogenies show evolutionary. Structure-function correlations and evolutionary adaptations dictate organismal survival and thermodynamic stability.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Cladistic Maximum Parsimony & Phylogenetic Inference**
  - **When to use**: Reconstructing evolutionary history using character state matrices
  - **How**: The most probable phylogenetic tree is the one requiring the fewest evolutionary character state changes (Occam's razor).
- **Serial Endosymbiosis & Eukaryotic Genesis**
  - **When to use**: Tracing the macroevolutionary transitions of primary and secondary plastids
  - **How**: Mitochondria derived from engulfed alpha-proteobacteria; chloroplasts derived from engulfed cyanobacteria, followed by secondary endosymbiotic uptake in diverse protist lineages.
- **Alternation of Generations & Plant Terrestrialization**
  - **When to use**: Comparing life cycles across bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms, and angiosperms
  - **How**: Progressive reduction of the independent gametophyte generation and dominance of the diploid sporophyte, accompanied by vascular tissues, seeds, and flowers.

## Key Concepts
### Concept 26.1: Phylogenies show evolutionary
- Linnaeus’s binomial classification system gives organisms twopart names: a genus plus a specific epithet.
- In the Linnaean system, species are grouped in increasingly broad taxa: Related genera are placed in the same family, families in orders, orders in classes, classes in phyla, phyla in kingdoms, and (more recently) kingdoms in domains.
- Systematists depict evolutionary relationships as branching phylogenetic trees. Many systematists propose that classification be based entirely on evolutionary relationships. Taxon A Taxon B Sister taxa Basal taxon Most recent common ancestor Branch point Taxon C Taxon D Taxon E Taxon F
- Unless branch lengths are proportional to time or genetic change, a phylogenetic tree indicates only patterns of descent.
- Much information can be learned about a species from its evolutionary history; hence, phylogenies are useful in a wide range of applications.

### Concept 26.2: Phylogenies are inferred from morphological
- Organisms with similar morphologies or DNA sequences are likely to be more closely related than organisms with very different structures and genetic sequences.
- To infer phylogeny, homology (similarity due to shared ancestry) must be distinguished from analogy (similarity due to convergent evolution).
- Computer programs are used to align comparable DNA sequences and to distinguish molecular homologies from coincidental matches between taxa that diverged long ago.

### Concept 26.3: Shared characters are used to construct
- A clade is a monophyletic group that includes an ancestral species and all of its descendants.

### Concept 26.4: An organism’s evolutionary history is
- Orthologous genes are homologous genes found in different species as a result of speciation. Paralogous genes are homologous genes within a species that result from gene duplication; such genes can diverge and potentially take on new functions.
- Distantly related species often have many orthologous genes. The small variation in gene number in organisms of varying complexity suggests that genes are versatile and may have multiple functions.

### Concept 26.5: Molecular clocks help track evolutionary
- Some regions of DNA change at a rate consistent enough to serve as a molecular clock, a method of estimating the date of past evolutionary events based on the amount of genetic change. Other DNA regions change in a less predictable way.
- Molecular clock analyses suggest that the most common strain of HIV jumped from primates to humans in the early 1900s.

### Concept 26.6: Our understanding of the tree of life
- Past classification systems have given way to the current view of the tree of life, which consists of three great domains: Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. ## Mental Models
- Think of 'Protista' not as a valid biological clade, but as a paraphyletic grab-bag of eukaryotic lineages belonging to distinct supergroups (Excavata, SAR, Archaeplastida, Unikonta).
- Use the animal body plan coordinate system (symmetry, germ layer triploblasty, coelom type, protostome vs deuterostome blastopore fate) to navigate animal diversity.
- Think of bacterial metabolic diversity as the ecological foundation of the biosphere (photoautotrophs, chemoautotrophs, photoheterotrophs, chemoheterotrophs).

## Anti-patterns
- **Viewing Evolution as a Linear Ladder of Progress (Scala Naturae)**: Modern species represent terminal branches on an ancient bush; humans did not evolve from modern chimpanzees, but share a common ancestor.
- **Confusing Fungal Hyphae with Plant Roots**: Fungi are opisthokont heterotrophs with chitinous cell walls that absorb nutrients via exoenzymes; their dikaryotic ($n+n$) stage is unique.
- **Equating Invertebrate with Monophyletic Group**: Invertebrates are defined by an absence of a backbone and comprise over 30 distinct animal phyla, representing >95% of animal diversity.
