## Core Idea
Plant Responses to Internal and External Signals establishes that biological function emerges from regulated interactions across scales: signal transduction pathways link signal. Structure-function correlations and evolutionary adaptations dictate organismal survival and thermodynamic stability.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Transpiration-Cohesion-Tension Xylem Transport**
  - **When to use**: Explaining long-distance negative-pressure water ascent in vascular plants
  - **How**: Evaporative water loss at stomata creates negative hydrostatic pressure (tension) in leaf mesophyll; hydrogen bonding cohesion transmits this pull downward through continuous xylem vessels.
- **Pressure-Flow Translocation in Phloem**
  - **When to use**: Explaining positive-pressure bulk flow of photoassimilates from source to sink
  - **How**: Active loading of sucrose at source tissues lowers water potential, drawing water into sieve tubes; high turgor pressure pushes sap toward sinks where sucrose is unloaded.
- **Phytochrome Photoperiodism & Hormonal Signaling Networks**
  - **When to use**: Regulating plant architecture, tropisms, dormancy, and floral induction
  - **How**: Phytochrome red/far-red light interconversion ($P_r ightleftharpoons P_{fr}$) measures night length; auxin polar transport establishes apical dominance and tropic bending.

### Concept 39.3: Responses to light are critical for plant
- Blue-light photoreceptors control hypocotyl elongation, stomatal opening, and phototropism.
- Phytochromes act like molecular “on-off” switches that regulate shade avoidance and germination of many seed types. Red light turns phytochrome “on,” and far-red light turns it “off.” ResponsesPr Pfr Red light Far-red light
- Phytochrome conversion also provides information about the day length (photoperiod) and hence the time of year. Photoperiodism regulates the time of flowering in many species. Short-day plants require a night longer than a critical length to flower. Long-day plants need a night length shorter than a critical period to flower.
- Many daily rhythms in plant behavior are controlled by an internal circadian clock. Free-running circadian rhythms are approximately 24 hours long but are entrained to exactly 24 hours by dawn and dusk effects on phytochrome form.

### Concept 39.4: Plants respond to a wide variety of stimuli
- Gravitropism is bending in response to gravity. Roots show positive gravitropism, and stems show negative gravitropism. Statoliths, starch-filled plastids, enable roots to detect gravity.
- Thigmotropism is a growth response to touch. Rapid leaf movements involve transmission of electrical impulses.
- Plants are sensitive to environmental stresses, including drought, flooding, high salinity, and extremes of temperature. ### Concept 39.5: Plants respond to attacks by pathogens and
- The hypersensitive response seals off an infection and destroys both pathogen and host cells in the region. Systemic acquired resistance is a generalized defense response in organs distant from the infection site.
- In addition to physical defenses such as thorns and trichomes, plants produce distasteful or toxic chemicals, as well as attractants that recruit animals that destroy herbivores.

## Mental Models
- Think of water potential $\Psi = \Psi_s + \Psi_p$ as a thermodynamic potential energy gradient; water ALWAYS flows spontaneously from higher $\Psi$ to lower $\Psi$.
- Use the primary meristem vs secondary meristem model: apical meristems lengthen shoots and roots; lateral meristems (vascular and cork cambium) thicken woody axes.
- Think of plant defense as a two-tier immune system: PAMP-triggered immunity (surface recognition) and Effector-triggered immunity (intracellular hypersensitive response & systemic acquired resistance).

## Anti-patterns
- **Believing Plants Push Water from Roots (Capillarity Fallacy)**: Root pressure rarely exceeds 0.2 MPa and cannot push water to treetop heights; transpiration pull generates negative pressures up to -2.0 MPa to lift water 100 meters.
- **Confusing Photoperiod Day Length with Night Length**: Flowering in short-day and long-day plants is strictly controlled by the duration of the UNINTERRUPTED dark period (critical night length).
- **Assuming All Plant Cells Have Chloroplasts**: Root cells, internal stem parenchyma, and floral petals lack functional chloroplasts and rely entirely on sucrose translocated from photosynthetic leaves.
